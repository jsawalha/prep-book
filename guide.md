## How to run a prep week

A simple rhythm that works with these recipes:

- **Sunday, about 90 minutes:** run two rice cooker bowls back to back, chopping for the second while the first cooks. Assemble both soup kits and put them in the freezer.
- **Monday to Wednesday:** eat the Sunday bowls. Fish goes first: [[One-Pot Miso Salmon Rice Bowl]] keeps 2 to 3 days, and the beef, chicken and turkey bowls keep 3 to 4.
- **Wednesday night, about 40 minutes, mostly hands-off:** one more rice cooker bowl, for Thursday and Friday.
- **Busy nights:** a soup kit straight from the freezer. It takes about 15 minutes from block to bowl.
- **Meatless option:** [[Garlic Tofu Veggie Rice]] makes 5 servings in one cycle. It works as a whole week of lunches or as a side for the protein-heavy bowls.
- **Weekend:** [[Kimchi Tuna Pancakes (Kimchi Jeon)]], made fresh. It's the one recipe here that doesn't hold up as a prep.

Every recipe makes 2 servings except the big KBBQ batch (8) and [[Garlic Tofu Veggie Rice]] (5). Use the servings control to set how many you want before adding a recipe to the shopping list.

## Mixing: recipes that share a shopping trip

Pairing recipes that share ingredients means less waste and a shorter list.

- **Both soup kits:** each uses half a block of tofu and 4 oz of thinly sliced chuck, so one block of tofu and 8 oz of beef covers both. They also use the same freezer method, so assemble them together.
- **The rice cooker rotation:** [[One-Pot Hainanese Chicken Rice]], [[Pad Kra Pao Turkey Rice Bowl]], [[One-Pot Beef Bulgogi Rice Bowl]], [[One-Pot Miso Salmon Rice Bowl]] and [[Spicy Bulgogi Lettuce Wrap Bowl]] all use ⅔ cup raw jasmine rice plus a splash of bone broth. Buy a big bag of jasmine rice and 1 L cartons of chicken and beef bone broth, and every week is covered. [[Garlic Tofu Veggie Rice]] uses the same jasmine rice, just a lot more of it (2.5 cups).
- **Korean pantry week:** [[Spicy Bulgogi Lettuce Wrap Bowl]], [[Spicy KBBQ Bulgogi Bowls (Big Batch)]] and [[Frozen Soup Kit #1: Gochujang Jjigae]] share gochujang, gochugaru, sesame oil, sesame seeds and green onions. Both bulgogi recipes use thinly sliced ribeye, which you can buy in one trip to a Korean market (often labeled "bulgogi meat").
- **Eggs:** [[Pad Kra Pao Turkey Rice Bowl]] and [[One-Pot Miso Salmon Rice Bowl]] each need 2 eggs, fried fresh. A half dozen covers both, with 2 left over.
- **Persian cucumbers:** Hainan chicken, pad kra pao and the KBBQ salad all use them. Persian cucumbers don't need peeling, and they stay crunchy longer than regular cucumbers.
- **Edamame:** the miso salmon bowl, the lettuce wrap bowl and the tofu veggie rice all use it. One bag of frozen shelled edamame covers all three.
- **Napa cabbage:** the hot pot kit and the miso salmon bowl both use it. One head covers both.

A good default week is **one freezer kit pair + two rice cooker bowls**. That's about 8 meals with very little overlap in flavor.

Easy ways to trim any of them without losing the dish:

- **Rice is the easiest lever.** ⅔ cup raw jasmine rice is about 450 calories per batch. Using ½ cup instead saves about 55 calories per serving. Make up the volume with more of the veg already in the recipe (napa, spinach, edamame, mushrooms).
- **Leaner beef.** Ribeye is the fattiest cut here. Swapping it for eye of round, the cut the plain bulgogi bowl uses, cuts a lot of fat for the same protein. It's leaner, so don't overcook it.
- **Watch the sweet stuff and oil.** Honey, brown sugar and sesame oil add up quickly in a big batch. Halving them changes the flavor less than you'd expect.
- **Going the other way (more food or a training day):** add rice. One extra cup of cooked rice adds about 45 g carbs and 200 calories.

The macros in the app are the creator's posted numbers. They don't change when you swap ingredients, so recalculate in a tracking app if you change a recipe a lot. This is general guidance, not dietitian advice.

## Cutting: knife and prep skills

- **Thin-slicing beef yourself:** freeze it for 30 to 45 minutes until firm but not solid, then slice across the grain about 3 mm thick. A Korean market will also slice it for you.
- **Chop in this order:** vegetables first, raw meat last. Or use a separate board for meat. Wash the board and knife in hot soapy water after the meat.
- **Onions:** halve them root to tip, peel, then slice with the grain for strips (bulgogi) or dice for soups.
- **Green onions:** white parts go into the cooking, green tops go on at the end for freshness.
- **Edamame:** the miso salmon bowl, the lettuce wrap bowl and the tofu veggie rice all use it. One bag of frozen shelled edamame covers all three.
- **Napa cabbage:** cut the thick white stems smaller than the leafy tops so they cook in the same time.
- **Garlic:** jarred minced garlic is fine here. About 1 tsp equals 1 clove.
- **Mushrooms (enoki, beech):** trim off the base, then separate them by hand. No knife work needed.
- **Kimchi:** snip it with kitchen scissors right in the bowl instead of on a cutting board.

## Storage and food safety

- **Cool fast.** Get cooked food into the fridge within 2 hours. Spreading rice out in shallow containers helps it cool.
- **Fridge:** most cooked bowls keep 3 to 4 days, and the meatless tofu rice keeps 4 to 5. Fish keeps 2 to 3, so eat it first.
- **Freezer:** cooked rice bowls (without eggs or lettuce) freeze well for about 2 months. Raw soup kits are best within about 3 months.
- **Thaw in the fridge, never on the counter.** The soup kits are designed to go straight from the freezer into boiling broth.
- **Cook to:** 74°C (165°F) for chicken and ground turkey, and 63°C (145°F) for salmon. Reheat leftovers until they're steaming hot all the way through, 74°C (165°F).
- **Keep these separate until you eat:** fried eggs (make them fresh), lettuce, cucumbers, chili sauce, and crunchy toppings like green onion and sesame.
